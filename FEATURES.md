# Features

Lattice builds a clash-free weekly class timetable for a college. One coordinator enters the data and generates the timetable, then prints or exports it. Everything is saved in the browser (`localStorage`). Signing in is optional. Signed in, you can create or join institutes, manage members as an institute's admin, save the timetable online to an institute so other members can open it, and publish it at a public link. Nothing goes online until someone clicks *Save online*.

The app walks through four steps: ① Setup week → ② Add data → ③ Generate → ④ View & export. Each step has a one-line hint, and non-obvious fields have ⓘ tooltips. On a narrow screen the stepper shows step numbers and the current step's name.

The header shows the Lattice logo (a 3×3 timetable grid with an L filled in) and name; the browser tab uses the same mark as its icon, a dark tile in a light browser and a light tile in a dark one (`web/public/logo.svg`, `logo-light.svg`).

## Account

- **Sign in (optional).** A **Sign in** button in the header opens a dialog with an unticked *I agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy* box (both linked, opening in a new tab), *Continue with Google* and *Email me a sign-in link* ("Check your inbox at …"). Both buttons stay disabled until the box is ticked. Signed in, the button becomes an avatar (Google's picture, or initials) whose menu shows your name (or email), *Help*, *Privacy*, *Terms* and **Sign out**. The session survives a reload.
- **Consent.** Every acceptance of the Terms and Privacy Policy is recorded with its version and time. The box ticked at sign-in is recorded when you come back from Google or the email link. Without that (an email link opened in another browser, or an account from before consent existed), a dialog *Before you continue* blocks the account until you choose *Accept* or *Sign out*; it has no close button and ignores Esc and outside clicks. When the policy version goes up, signed-in users get the same dialog titled *We've updated our Terms and Privacy Policy*, with a *What changed* list. Nothing else happens to the account, including joining institutes from invites, until it is accepted.
- **Signed out is the full app.** Signing in or out never touches the timetable data in the browser. Without `web/.env.local` (set up by `scripts/setup-auth.sh`) there is no Sign in button at all.
- An expired or reused email link shows a toast asking for a new one.
- **Institutes.** On every sign-in, pending invites for your email are accepted at once, with a toast like "You joined GNDEC Ludhiana as editor". With no institute and no invite, a *Create institute* dialog asks for a name, with *Not now* (it then won't open by itself again in that browser). The avatar menu shows the active institute, your role and its admin ("Editor · admin is Dr. Meera Iyer (meera@…)"), and *Create institute…*. With two or more institutes it lists them to switch; the choice is remembered per browser, falling back to the first institute if it's gone.
- **Roles.** Each institute has exactly one admin, plus editors and viewers. Editors and viewers see their role and who the admin is, and can *Leave institute* (after a confirm). Only the admin sees *Members…*, *Rename…*, *Link name…* and *Delete institute…* (type the name to confirm). Roles are enforced by the database; a refusal shows as a plain-words toast such as "Only the admin can do that."
- **Link name (admin).** The institute's part of public links (`gndec` in `/gndec/k7f3q9xa2m`), set from the avatar menu's *Link name…* or when first publishing: 3–30 lowercase letters, digits or hyphens, not starting or ending with a hyphen, a few words reserved (including `help`, checked as you type). It is checked as you type, with a preview of the link; capitals and outer spaces are forgiven. A name another institute has gets "That handle is taken." Changing it later keeps old links working.
- **Members (admin).** The admin first, then everyone else with name, email and a role select (editor/viewer), *Make admin* (hands the role over; you become an editor) and *Remove*. Pending invites have *Cancel*. The invite form takes an email and a role, says to use the address they'll sign in with, and rejects a malformed address, an existing member or one already invited before calling the database. No invite email is sent; tell them to sign in.
- **Online timetables.** A status line in the header, left of the avatar, shows where the working copy stands: "On this device only", or the linked online timetable and its institute ("CSE odd sem 2026 · GNDEC"), plus "· published" when its public link shows a version, and "· unsaved changes" (with an amber dot) or "· view only" for viewers. On a narrow screen only the icon shows. Its menu:
  - *Save online*: the first time, a dialog picks the institute (where you are admin or editor, starting with the active one) and the name (the print heading, else "Timetable"), then links the working copy to the new timetable. After that it adds a version. If someone saved since you opened it: "Changed online by A at 10:42 since you opened it" with *Overwrite*, *Save as new timetable…* or *Cancel*. If the timetable was deleted or you lost edit access, it says so and offers *Save as new timetable…*. A network error is a toast; nothing changes.
  - *Save as new timetable…* (name defaults to "<name> (copy)"), *Open from <institute>…* (name, latest version, who saved it and when, a *Live* badge for published ones; opening over unsaved work asks *Replace your working copy?*), *Rename…* (admin and editors), *Delete…* (admin, type the name; drops the link, keeps the working copy) and *Work on this device only* (drops the link, keeps the data).
  - *Publish* (admin and editors) points the timetable's public link at the linked version and shows the link (`<site>/<handle>/<id>`) with *Copy* and *Open*. With unsaved changes it asks *Save online first?* (*Save and publish*). Without a generated timetable it is greyed out, with the reason as a tooltip. If the institute has no link name yet, the admin picks one there and then and publishing carries on; an editor is told "Ask your admin (X) to set the institute's link name". Publishing again updates what the link shows; the link never changes.
  - *Versions…* (also a *Versions* button on each row of *Open from <institute>*) lists the kept versions newest first, "Version 7 · Meera · 24 Oct, 10:42", with a *Live* badge and the public link above the list. *Open* loads a version into the working copy (asking first over unsaved work); an older one opens as unsaved changes, so *Save online* restores it as the newest version with no conflict. *Make live* (admin and editors) switches the link to that version at once, after "The public link will show version 5"; *Unpublish…* takes the link down until the next publish. Viewers see the list and *Open* only.
  - Viewers can open timetables but get no write actions. Signed out, the line stays and *Save online* says to sign in. *Start over* drops the link; everything else (import, sample, edits, generate) only marks unsaved changes. The link survives a reload. The step stays on the device.
- **Email confirmation.** *Make admin* and *Leave institute* need you to have signed in by email link in the last 10 minutes; the database refuses them otherwise. If you haven't, a *Confirm from your email* dialog emails a sign-in link to your own address (Google users too). Opening it, in the same tab or a new one, signs you in again and asks *Make X the admin of Y?* (or *Leave Y?*) once more before doing it.
- **Assistant.** An *Assistant* button in the header (icon only on a phone) opens a panel on the right, full screen on a phone, on every step; the page makes room so the steps stay usable, and Esc or ✕ closes it. It answers questions about using Lattice and about the working copy (unsaved edits included), in the language you write in, and declines anything else. While it works it shows "Thinking…" and what it looked at ("Looked at CSE A's week"); answers link to the help sections they use, in a new tab. Asked to change something, it shows a **change card** instead (see the next bullet). What the panel shows: signed out, *Sign in to use the assistant* with the sign-in button; no institute, *Create an institute…*; an institute without access, a short description and, for the admin, a coupon box (forgiving of case, spaces and dashes; "That coupon isn't valid." for a wrong or used code; the database's words for too many tries) and *Request a trial coupon* (a dialog with the email to `VITE_CONTACT_EMAIL`, the institute's name, link name and the question filled in: *Open in Gmail* opens Gmail's full-page compose in a new tab, from the browser's first Google account, *Open email app* is the `mailto:` and comes first on touch screens, and To, Subject and Message each have *Copy*, which shows ✓ Copied, with a line to copy by hand if the clipboard fails), or for editors and viewers "Your institute's admin, Asha (asha@…), can unlock it."; a monthly pause or a server without the key, a plain line. Unlocked, the header reads "143 questions left · until 23 Nov", amber under 20 questions or 3 days, updated after every answer; when they run out the panel locks again and keeps the chat on screen. The institute is the one the working copy is linked to, else a picker in the panel (hidden with one institute). Enter sends, Shift+Enter adds a line, *New chat* clears it; the chat lives only in the tab and is summarised every 20 questions so it can go on. Teacher names are replaced by codes before anything is sent and shown as names again on screen. Signed in, *Ask about this* on each *Can't be built* reason, the *Couldn't finish* card, each group of unplaced classes and each data check in step ② opens the panel and asks about that card.
- **Assistant change cards.** Asked to change something, the assistant proposes it as a card in the chat ("Changes #3"): a summary, one line per change with teachers by name ("Move CSE A Data Structures from Mon P3 to Tue P1 · CSE Room B · locked", "Save online as version 7 of “CSE odd sem 2026”"), any new step ② data checks the changes cause, an amber warning on a change that clears the timetable (a new week) or would lose unsaved work (*Load sample*, opening a timetable), "Undo restores your working copy; the saved version stays online" when it has online changes, then **Apply**, **Discard** and *How cards work*. It can propose: add, change or remove departments, teachers, rooms, sections and courses; a new week; lock, unlock, move and swap classes (moved classes are locked there); switch *What to aim for* rules on or off ("Turn on: Finish each day as early as possible"); regenerate; load the sample; open a timetable from the institute; and, for admins and editors, *Save online*, *Save as new*, rename, publish and *Make live*. Every proposal is dry-run in the browser first (moves keep H1–H8, online changes are checked against the role and the versions); one that fails goes back to the model to fix and shows no card. **Apply** checks the card again on the working copy as it is now ("Out of date: ask again" if it no longer fits, and nothing changes; older cards that still fit can be applied), takes an undo snapshot ("Undo: assistant changes #3"), makes the working-copy changes, runs Generate exactly as the button does and shows "Score 34 → 28 · 0 unplaced" ("Score 34 → 41 with Late finish on · …" when the card switched a rule) (or why it failed, with *See why*), then makes the online changes in order. Each step gets a tick; the first failure stops it with the reason under that step ("Stopped: someone saved first. Save by hand." for a conflict), and the steps before it stay done. A card is applied or discarded once; one card applies at a time, and not while a question runs. What happened goes to the assistant at the start of the next question ("[Applied changes #3]", "[Applied changes #3 up to step 5: save conflict]", "[Discarded changes #3]"); *Apply* and *Discard* use no question. Deleting, unpublishing, invites, members, roles, the link name, *Overwrite* and a save that would drop a version stay manual: the assistant gives the steps and the help link.
- **Help pages.** A *Help* link under every step (next to *Privacy* and *Terms*) and in the account menu opens `/help` in a new tab: an index listing each page with its sections, then `/help/start`, `/data`, `/generate`, `/timetable`, `/online` and `/troubleshooting`. They are plain pages like the policy ones, readable without JavaScript, on a phone and printed, with a contents list on each page and a link for every section (e.g. `/help/data#lab-batches`). `/help/start` is a five-minute tutorial on the sample college with screenshots; the other pages explain each step, every *Can't be built* cause and unplaced-class group, and the common problems. The app links into them, always in a new tab: *Help* after each step's hint, *What this means* on each cause (also under *Before you start*), on *Couldn't finish* and on each group of unplaced classes, *What these mean* on import errors, *What these checks mean* under step ②'s problems, *What to do* in the solver-offline banner, and *Learn more* in the ⓘ tooltips where the help says more (periods, lunch candidates, rooms, lab batches, load, the exact solver, the score). `/help/online` covers signing in, institutes and a table of what admin, editor and viewer can each do, saving online, a save clashing with someone else's, versions, publishing and the link name, the assistant (what it can do, coupons, the allowance, privacy), and leaving or deleting, with screenshots; `/help/troubleshooting` covers a sign-in email that didn't arrive, "Only the admin can do that", "Changed online by …" and the consent dialog. Help screenshots show at their true size, not doubled. *Learn more* links open the matching section from the *Save online*, *Can't save*, conflict, *Versions*, *Published*, link name, *Your institute needs a link name*, *Create an institute* and *Members* dialogs (*What each role can do* there), and *Didn't get it?* follows "Check your inbox" in the sign-in and *Confirm from your email* dialogs. The text comes from `web/src/help/*.md`.
- **Privacy Policy and Terms.** *Privacy* and *Terms* links under every step open `/privacy` and `/terms` in a new tab: plain pages with the Lattice header and *Back to Lattice*, readable without JavaScript and printable on A4. Version 2 covers saving timetables online (what is stored, who sees it, the last 20 versions kept, teacher data) and publishing them at a public link. Version 3 adds the assistant: what it sends to Anthropic (teacher names as codes), that chats aren't stored, and the usage and coupon records; the Terms add that its answers can be wrong and that trial coupons are free and can be withdrawn. Version 4 says where the deployed app runs: our server on Amazon Web Services in Mumbai (request logs with no timetable data or IP address, kept 14 days), Cloudflare serving the site and forwarding the contact address, and Resend, a US company, sending sign-in emails. The text comes from `web/src/legal/privacy.md` and `terms.md`, with the version, effective date, operator and contact address (`VITE_CONTACT_EMAIL`) filled in at build time; without that variable the pages say "(contact address not set)".

## ① Setup week

- **Working days.** Pick any days from Mon to Sun. A new setup starts with Mon–Fri ticked and no periods.
- **Periods.** Edit each period's label, start, end and kind (teaching or break), or fill the table with **Generate periods** from a start time, length and count. The form is open while there are no periods. Every day uses the same periods.
- **Lunch candidates.** Mark the teaching periods where lunch may fall. Every section and every teacher gets at least one of them free each day.
- **Inline checks.** A period with an empty label, missing times, an end before its start, or a start before the previous period ends is highlighted. The step also needs at least one working day, one teaching period and one lunch candidate.

Changing any setting clears the current timetable and its locks, because placements refer to day and period positions.

## ② Add data

- **Sample college.** One click loads 3 departments, 9 sections, 25 teachers and the courses behind 210 sessions.
- **Excel template and import.** Download a workbook with one sheet per table (Departments, Teachers, Rooms, Sections, Courses), fill it in, and upload it. A CSV per sheet works too. An import with any error is rejected as a whole and lists the problems by row, e.g. `Courses row 7: teacher "Sharma" not found`. A valid import asks before it replaces the current data.
- **Export.** Download the current data as `.xlsx` in the same template format.
- **Edit tables.** Tabs for Departments, Rooms, Teachers, Sections and Courses, in the order the data depends on, with add, edit and delete. The step opens on Departments while there is no data and on Courses once courses exist. Courses can be filtered by section (the same searchable picker as step ④) and by teacher (a teacher's courses include those where they only take one batch's lab); the filters reset on reload.
- **Rooms.** A room is a classroom or a lab. A room with no department is shared by everyone; a room with a department serves only that department. A lab course can require one specific lab.
- **Lab batches.** A section can split into batches. Each batch gets its own lab sessions, batches can be in different labs at the same time, and each batch can have its own lab teacher. In the Courses table, the batch teachers cell lists only the batches with their own teacher and opens a small form with one picker per batch.
- **Data checks.** The summary shows counts, load bars for the busiest sections, and two kinds of problems (each with *Ask about this* when signed in). Missing references, a session with no eligible room, or a lab longer than any run of consecutive teaching periods block **Next**. A section or teacher booked for more periods than the week has gives a warning, and so does a room pool booked beyond its periods: a lab that courses require, the classrooms or labs one department may use (its own plus shared ones), or all rooms of a type.

## ③ Generate

- **Exact solver only.** **Generate timetable** asks the exact solver (OR-Tools CP-SAT on the Python server). It finds the best timetable or proves none exists; the sample college solves to the optimum (score 18) in about 10 s. There is no engine picker and no silent fallback: the browser's quick draft runs only from a button that says *quick draft* or *partial*. The browser replaces each teacher's name and id with a code such as `T-K3X9Q` before sending the data (an imported id is a slug of the name), so the solver never receives either; a solve may take the time limit plus 15 s before the page gives up; the server logs one line per request (method, path, status, duration), never its data or the visitor's address.
- **Server status.** The site checks the solver once as it opens, on any step, which wakes a sleeping server before step ③. A dot reads *Exact solver: online / offline* and is re-checked every 5 s; each check waits up to 5 s, long enough for a cold start. If the server runs a different contract version (`shared/contract.json`) or doesn't say which, step ③ shows a grey line under the dot's card: "The solver was updated at a different time from this page. Reload the page; if this stays, results may be wrong.", and the console logs both numbers. While it is offline an amber banner says a quick draft will be made in the browser instead (in dev builds it also shows the command that starts the server), and the main button reads **Make a quick draft**. When the server comes back the banner goes and the button returns to *Generate timetable*.
- **Quick draft.** A heuristic in a Web Worker: it places the most constrained sessions first, restarts with different random seeds, then improves the result by moving and swapping sessions. On the sample it places all 210 sessions with score 18 in about 1.5 s (it uses at most 5 s).
- **Before you start.** Section, teacher and room loads that exceed the week (from step ②'s checks) are listed above the button with a fix link each. They never block Generate.
- **What to aim for.** A card above the Generate card with one switch per soft rule, phrased as an aim: *Spread each subject across the week* (S1), *At most 3 periods in a row for a teacher* (S2), *No free periods between a section's classes* (S3), *Keep a section's batches busy at the same time* (S4), *Finish each day as early as possible* (S5), each with an ⓘ giving its penalty and its name in the score. Flipping one keeps the timetable and locks, re-scores the timetable at once (steps ③ and ④), and shows "Preferences changed: regenerate to use them" next to the main button until the next run (or until the switches are flipped back). A flip by hand takes no undo step. The switches are disabled while a run is going.
- **Time limit.** No setting: Generate gives the exact solver 20 s. While a run is going the button reads *Generating…* with "7 s of up to 20 s". When a *Try again with 40 s* or *Try longer: 40 s* run finishes with a timetable, later runs use that limit until a reload.
- **Cancel.** **Cancel** next to *Generating…* (or *Regenerating…* in step ④) stops the run and never starts a quick draft; the previous timetable stays.
- **One main button.** **Generate timetable** is the main button until a timetable exists; then **Next: View & export** takes over and Regenerate turns into an outlined button. **Next** stays disabled until there is a timetable and while a run is going. A finished run scrolls its result into view.
- **Timetable ready.** A green card: *best possible* when the solver proved the optimum, or *best found in 20 s* with **Try longer: 40 s** (doubling, up to 120 s).
- **Can't be built.** When the solver proves the data impossible, a red card says so (and that nothing was changed), with the top 5 causes and *+N more*: classes with no room or no long enough run of periods, locks in slots they can't use, locks that make it impossible (re-checked without them for up to 5 s), then section, teacher and room loads by how far over they are. Each cause has a fix button that opens step ② on the right tab, with the Courses filter set to the section or teacher, or step ④ to clear the locks. Signed in, each cause also has *Ask about this* for the assistant. With no single cause it says the rules conflict in combination and lists general suggestions. **Make a partial timetable** below makes a quick draft that places what fits.
- **Couldn't finish.** When the solver runs out of time, can't be reached or fails, a neutral card says what happened and that the previous timetable is unchanged. A timeout offers **Try again with 40 s** (hidden at 120 s); every kind offers *Make a quick draft instead*, and *Ask about this* when signed in.
- **Partial timetable.** An amber card: "Partial: 17 of 237 classes not placed", how it was made, 0 clashes among placed classes, and the unplaced classes grouped by cause (teacher busy, section busy, no free room, no room they may use, lab too long, last lunch period) in collapsible groups, each with *What this means* and, signed in, *Ask about this*. After *Can't be built* it has **Back to the reasons**. Next is allowed.
- **Verification badge.** A TypeScript validator re-checks every result against the hard rules, whichever engine produced it: `✓ Timetable ready · 0 clashes (independently checked)`, or `Partial: N classes not placed · 0 clashes among placed classes`. The score is also recomputed in the browser, so both engines are scored by the same code. Each soft rule shows its points and what was counted, e.g. "Batches waiting during labs · 18 pts · 6 batch-periods", with the same wording in steps ③ and ④; a rule that's switched off reads *Off*, and the ⓘ next to the total lists the rules it counts.
- **Wording.** Coordinators read "Exact solver" and "Quick draft"; "CP-SAT" appears only in ⓘ tooltips.

## ④ View & export

- **Timetable grid.** One week grid with a Section / Teacher / Room switcher, a searchable picker grouped by department, and previous/next buttons. Labs span their periods, each subject has its own colour (subjects in one section get colours as far apart as the palette allows), and parallel batch labs stack in one cell. The break column reads BREAK and shows its times on hover.
- **Session details.** Click a class for subject, section, batch, teacher, room and time. Screen readers read the whole class from the card.
- **Lock and regenerate.** The lock button in a card's corner, or **Lock here** in the details dialog, pins a session to its slot (shown with 🔒 and a thick border). The button appears on hover or keyboard focus and stays visible on locked classes. **Regenerate (keep N locked)**, in steps ③ and ④, re-solves everything else with the exact solver (while it is offline the button reads *Regenerate as quick draft*). A Regenerate that fails keeps the timetable and toasts "Can't be built with these locks." (or "Couldn't finish in time.") with *See why →* to step ③'s card; while the failure stands, a red line above the grid offers **Clear all locks** and *See why →*. Locks that no longer fit the data, such as a deleted room or two locks that clash, are dropped with a warning before solving. **Clear all locks** removes them all.
- **Drag and drop.** In the Section view, drag a class to another slot. Free slots turn green and show how the score would change; the rest turn red, and the dragged card says why (e.g. "Dr. Meera Iyer teaches CSE B Database Systems at Tue P3"). A lab grabbed by its second period lands one period earlier, so it goes where it looks. A drop is re-checked by the validator, then the class is moved, locked there and the score updated, with an **Undo** in the toast (the same undo as the header's, while the move is still the newest change). Unplaced sessions can be dragged onto the grid the same way. Step ④ shows the verified badge, so you can see the timetable is still clash-free.
- **Undo and Redo.** Header arrows next to *Start over*, shown only when there is something to undo or redo, with tooltips naming the change ("Undo: move CSE A Operating Systems to Mon P8", "Undo: assistant changes #3") and **Ctrl+Z** / **Ctrl+Shift+Z** (⌘ on a Mac) outside text fields. Each drag-and-drop move and each applied assistant card snapshots the data, settings, result and locks first; an undo restores that snapshot and a toast says what was undone. Up to 20 changes; a new change clears Redo. Both wait while Generate runs.
- **Stats panel.**
  - Quality score as one line under the verified badge: the total and a chip per soft rule, amber when the rule costs points.
  - Room utilisation bars across the full width in two columns, with a headline figure for shared rooms.
  - A sortable teacher load table with per-day bars (labelled Mo, Tu, We…) and Highest/Lowest badges. It shows the top 10 teachers, with **Show all** for the rest.
- **Partial banner.** While the timetable has unplaced classes, an amber banner above the grid says how many and that they can be dragged in; the badge turns amber too.
- **Print / PDF.** **Print** is the main button in step ④. It opens a small panel with the heading printed at the top (e.g. college and semester, saved with the data) and two choices: **This timetable** prints the current view, **All sections** prints one A4 landscape page per section. Subject colours are kept and app controls, toasts and tooltips are hidden. Use the browser's *Save as PDF* to get a file. With unplaced classes the panel warns that they won't appear on printouts.
- **CSV.** The CSV button next to Print (with the outlined Regenerate) downloads every placement as one row: section, batch, day, periods, time, subject, kind, teacher, room. It opens cleanly in Excel.

## Public timetable page

- **A link anyone can open.** `<site>/<handle>/<id>` (e.g. `/gndec/k7f3q9xa2m`) shows a published timetable without signing in. The header has the institute name, the timetable name, the print heading and "Updated 24 Oct 2026" (when the live version was saved).
- **Section, Teacher and Room views** with the same picker (search, previous, next) and grids as step ④, read-only: no locks, no dragging, no stats. Clicking a class opens its details.
- **On a phone** (below 640 px) it shows one day at a time as a list of classes (time, periods, subject, teacher or section, room) with day tabs, opening on today, or the first day when today isn't a teaching day.
- **Print** uses step ④'s A4 landscape layout for the current view.
- An old link keeps working after the institute changes its handle; the address bar switches to the new one. An unknown or unpublished id says "This timetable isn't published."; without the Supabase env vars, or when the request fails, "Couldn't load this timetable. Try again later."
- Marked `noindex` for search engines, with no analytics. The page loads none of the sign-in or account code.

## Scheduling rules

Hard rules, which a valid timetable never breaks:

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| H1 | A teacher is in one session at a time. |
| H2 | A section is in one session at a time. A whole-section class clashes with every batch; two batch labs clash only if they are the same batch. |
| H3 | A room hosts one session at a time. |
| H4 | The room type matches the session, the room is shared or owned by the section's department, and a lab that requires a specific room gets it. |
| H5 | Every session is placed exactly once, or listed as unplaced with a reason. |
| H6 | A lab fills consecutive teaching periods on one day and never spans a break. |
| H7 | Nothing is placed in a break. |
| H8 | Every section and every teacher has a free lunch-candidate period each day. |

Soft rules, which add to the score (lower is better):

| ID | Rule | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | The same subject lectured more than once a day | 10 per extra lecture |
| S2 | A teacher teaching more than 3 periods in a row | 5 per period beyond 3 |
| S3 | Idle periods in a section's day, not counting lunch candidates | 2 per period |
| S4 | One batch idle while another batch of the section is in a lab | 3 per idle batch-period |
| S5 | Free periods before a section's or teacher's last class of a day, one free lunch candidate excused | 1 per period |

Each soft rule can be switched off (`softRules` in the working copy, saved with the data and online versions); a rule that's off shows "Off" in the score and adds nothing, and both engines ignore it. The switches are the *What to aim for* card in step ③. S1–S4 are on by default and S5 is off, since aiming for it made the exact solver's S1–S4 much worse within 20 s on 2 threads. With S5 on, both engines aim for it.

## Known limits

- With Late finish (S5) on, the exact solver does much worse on the other rules within 20 s on 2 threads (the sample: S1–S4 240–260 instead of 56–108), which is why it starts off. The quick draft has no such cost: on the sample it still reaches 18 on S1–S4 and cuts late finish from about 400 to about 100.
- The heuristic runs until its time limit unless it reaches score 0, and its results vary slightly between runs.
- The exact solver needs about 2 s before its first solution on the sample, so very short limits end in *Couldn't finish*; heavily over-booked data can also take longer than 5 s to prove impossible.
- A *Can't be built* or *Couldn't finish* card stays until the next run, even after the data is fixed.
- A complete quick draft gets no special label and no *Improve with exact solver* button.
- A lock that blocks every other slot shows up as unplaced sessions rather than a dropped lock.
- Printouts don't show the 🔒 marker.
- Cancelling a CP-SAT run only stops the browser waiting. The server keeps solving until its time limit and its answer is ignored.
- Sign-in emails come from Supabase's built-in sender, which reaches only the Supabase project's team members, 2 emails an hour. Google sign-in works only for accounts listed as test users in Google Cloud. Both open up in the deploy pass.
- Viewers can still edit the working copy in their own browser; they just can't save it online. Someone else's save shows up only when you open the timetable or save (the conflict check), not live, and two editors' changes are never merged.
- The email confirmation is a link, not a 6-digit code, because the built-in sender's templates can't be edited on the free plan. Like sign-in emails, it reaches only the Supabase team, 2 an hour; both change in the deploy pass.
- Changes made by someone else (a new role, a removal, a deleted institute) show up when you next open the account menu, not live.
- The Privacy Policy and Terms haven't been reviewed by a lawyer.
- *Learn more* in an ⓘ tooltip can be clicked with a mouse but not reached by keyboard; the step hints' *Help* links cover keyboard users.
- A policy bump needs a migration row plus `POLICY_VERSION` and `POLICY_CHANGES` in `web/src/legal/policy.ts`; the app and database must agree, or no one can accept the new version.
- Undo and Redo live in memory, so a reload clears them. Only drag-and-drop moves and applied assistant cards go on the list: table edits, week changes, *What to aim for* switches flipped by hand and Generate on its own don't, yet an undo restores the whole working copy, so a table edit made after a move is undone with it. Undo never touches the saved online version, so a card's saves, renames and publishing stay done online.
- Drag and drop works in the Section view only, with a mouse; there is no keyboard dragging (use *Lock here* instead).
- The version list doesn't show which versions have a generated timetable; *Make live* on one without refuses with a toast.
- In the dev server only, a link name of `src`, `public` or `node_modules` would point at the server's own folders instead of the public page.
- The assistant changes things only through a card the user applies, and *Apply* takes the whole card: to drop one change, ask again. Deleting, unpublishing, invites, members, roles, the link name, *Overwrite* after a conflict and a save that would drop an old version stay manual. A new teacher's name typed in a question ("add teacher Ravi Sharma") goes to Anthropic as typed, since it isn't in the data yet. A card's online steps can't be undone; a step that fails leaves the earlier ones done. A card is checked against the working copy only on *Apply*, not again against the online state, so a save that became a conflict meanwhile stops at that step. At most 100 changes per card. Access comes only from trial coupons (`server/scripts/issue_coupon.py`); there is no paid plan. Its answers can be wrong. *Open in Gmail* sends from the browser's first Google account, not necessarily the one signed in to Lattice, and Gmail's full-page compose doesn't show which account it is. A misspelt or partial teacher name in a question isn't replaced by a code, so it reaches Anthropic. Answers arrive whole, not streamed, after several seconds of "Thinking…". The chat is gone after a reload.
- The public page doesn't remember which section or teacher you picked; it opens on the first section each time. It has no *Print all sections*.
